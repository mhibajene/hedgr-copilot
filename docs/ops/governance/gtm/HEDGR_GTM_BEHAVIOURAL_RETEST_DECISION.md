## Problem / Trigger

The first `GTM-MARKET-DISCOVERY-001` evidence cycle did not establish sufficient evidence to select Ghana, Kenya or the Philippines as Hedgr's launch market.

The cycle instead exposed a more fundamental uncertainty:

> Does Hedgr solve a recurring stability-decision problem, or are the observed needs primarily transaction, payout, FX-timing or money-movement problems?

The first evidence cycle identified a plausible recurring decision among already-earning freelancers:

> When money becomes available, how much should be spent, retained, converted or allocated without weakening financial stability?

Subsequent Founder/HedgrOps deliberation identified a related hypothesis: regularly salaried young professionals may face the same underlying decision grammar despite different income structures.

This creates a material product-convergence question that should be resolved before further market selection, provider diligence or GTM expansion.

---

## Decision

Authorise, subject to repo-native activation, **one bounded Lane G behavioural-market retest**.

The retest will determine whether:

1. already-earning freelancers / independent professionals; and
2. a narrowly defined salaried-young-professional comparison cohort

exhibit the same recurring Stability Engine decision grammar strongly enough to support a common product thesis.

Freelancers remain the **anchor cohort**.

Young professionals are admitted as a **research comparison cohort only**. Their inclusion does not constitute an expansion of the canonical MVP target audience unless supported by a later Founder disposition.

Ghana, Kenya and the Philippines remain the existing contextual market hypotheses. No new country is added by this decision.

The retest will prioritise the following primary behavioural hypothesis:

> **When money becomes available, the user has a consequential unresolved decision about what should remain liquid, be spent, retained, converted or allocated.**

A secondary comparison hypothesis will also be tested:

> **Before making a meaningful financial commitment, the user needs to understand whether that decision weakens their financial stability.**

The retest will not attempt to select the best country for Hedgr.

Its purpose is to determine whether a sufficiently coherent **behavioural market** exists for the Stability Engine.

---

## Rationale

The first Lane G cycle showed that financial infrastructure alone cannot establish Hedgr product demand.

Strong payment and payout infrastructure can simultaneously:

- enable Hedgr;
- reduce friction to future execution; and
- provide strong substitutes that may reduce Hedgr's incremental value.

Accordingly, the next uncertainty should be resolved in the following order:

> **Recurring decision → interpretation gap → behavioural consequence → existing alternative → market / rail conditions**

This preserves the Stability Engine as the product centre.

It avoids allowing available rails, stablecoins, payout methods or cross-border infrastructure to manufacture a product use case that users do not actually experience.

Testing a narrowly defined salaried comparison cohort provides a reversible way to determine whether the original freelancer/cross-border wedge reflects:

- a specific freelancer problem; or
- a more general stability/allocation decision shared across income structures.

The comparison is therefore intended to **falsify or validate a common decision grammar**, not broaden Hedgr's audience by assumption.

---

## Target Research Population

### Anchor cohort

Already-earning freelancers or independent professionals who:

- receive recurring earned income;
- use digital financial tools;
- make meaningful decisions about spending, retaining, converting or allocating money;
- have sufficient financial agency to act on an insight.

### Comparison cohort

Already-earning salaried young professionals who:

- receive recurring earned income;
- routinely use digital banking or wallet tools;
- retain some surplus, buffer or savings capacity after essential obligations;
- make repeated decisions about spending versus retaining or allocating money;
- can act on the decision being studied.

### Out of scope

The retest does not target users whose dominant problem is:

- lack of income;
- basic financial access;
- unemployment or income creation;
- credit access;
- debt distress;
- general remittance receipt;
- comprehensive budgeting;
- broad financial education;
- investment management.

---

## Research Questions

The retest should establish, through recent concrete financial episodes:

### Trigger
What happened immediately before the decision?

Examples may include:

- salary receipt;
- freelance/client payment;
- upcoming bill;
- large purchase;
- FX movement;
- savings milestone.

### Current behaviour
How does the user currently decide what to do?

What accounts, tools, heuristics or informal rules are used?

### Interpretation gap
What remains unclear after existing banks, wallets, payout products or spreadsheets have done their jobs?

### Decision consequence
Would clearer stability interpretation materially change what the user does?

### Recurrence
Does this class of decision occur often enough to create repeat behaviour?

---

## Evidence That Would Strengthen the Thesis

Evidence should strengthen the common behavioural-market hypothesis where repeated observations show that:

- users possess money but lack confidence about allocation;
- balances and transaction tools do not adequately explain decision consequences;
- users manually coordinate financial information across multiple tools or accounts;
- users maintain informal liquidity or buffer rules;
- users delay or modify financial actions while determining what is safe;
- stability interpretation materially influences action;
- the decision recurs;
- freelancers and salaried earners exhibit materially similar reasoning despite different income structures.

A particularly strong result would be:

> **Different income patterns and financial rails, but the same underlying stability-decision model.**

---

## Evidence That Would Weaken the Thesis

The common behavioural-market hypothesis should weaken where evidence shows that:

- users already know what they want to do and mainly need execution;
- the dominant problem is cheaper or faster transfers;
- the dominant problem is FX timing;
- the dominant salaried-user need is conventional budgeting;
- existing banking or wallet tools already provide sufficient decision support;
- Hedgr interpretation does not materially change behaviour;
- the two cohorts require materially different product logic;
- the relevant decisions occur too infrequently to support recurring use.

Negative evidence must lead to narrowing, revision or stopping rather than further audience expansion.

## Assumptions

The retest proceeds on the following reversible assumptions:

1. A recurring financial-stability decision may exist independently of cross-border money movement.
2. Freelancers remain a credible anchor cohort for testing this behaviour.
3. Salaried young professionals may provide a useful falsification/comparison case without becoming an MVP target by default.
4. Recent concrete financial decisions will provide stronger evidence than stated product preference.
5. Existing financial tools may solve execution while leaving an unresolved interpretation problem.
6. Ghana, Kenya and the Philippines remain sufficient contextual environments for this retest; no geographic expansion is currently necessary.
7. Behavioural research can be conducted without widening financial execution authority.

These assumptions remain hypotheses until supported by evidence.

## Risks / Trade-offs

### Cohort expansion risk

Introducing young professionals could silently broaden the MVP audience.

**Control:** classify them explicitly as a comparison cohort only.

### Product-thesis dilution

The research could drift from financial stability into budgeting, payments or generic personal finance.

**Control:** retain the recurring stability-decision question as the research centre and apply explicit stop conditions.

### Confirmation bias

Researchers may attempt to demonstrate that both cohorts fit the Stability Engine.

**Control:** actively seek cases where the decision does not exist or existing tools are sufficient.

### Market-selection leakage

Kenya's clearer behavioural pathway could be misinterpreted as a preferred launch market.

**Control:** use Kenya as a behavioural clarity case only; no country ranking or launch selection follows from the retest.

### Cross-lane contamination

Existing product or participant evidence could be reused beyond its authorised purpose.

**Control:** no participant, data or evidence reuse without explicit governing authority.

### Research without convergence

The retest could become another open-ended discovery cycle.

**Control:** require one terminal disposition at cycle close.

## Stop / Kill Criteria

Stop or narrow the affected research if:

- observed need is primarily cheaper or faster money movement;
- observed need is primarily FX timing;
- the salaried cohort's dominant need is generic budgeting;
- Hedgr interpretation does not materially affect decisions;
- the two cohorts require different core product logic;
- participants do not experience the decision often enough to support recurring behaviour;
- research begins implying live financial capability;
- sensitive data requirements exceed authorised research controls;
- participant access or evidence is obtained outside approved protocol;
- research scope expands to additional markets or user groups without new authority;
- evidence volume increases without progressing toward a terminal disposition.

---

## Required Terminal Disposition

The retest must conclude with one of four dispositions:

### RETAIN
The existing freelancer / cross-currency wedge remains materially stronger and should remain the primary behavioural market.

### BROADEN
Evidence supports a common behavioural market across freelancers and salaried earners, justifying separate Founder consideration of a broader target-user definition.

### SPLIT
Both cohorts demonstrate meaningful needs, but the underlying decision grammars are materially different and should not be treated as one MVP market.

### STOP / REVISE
Neither cohort demonstrates a sufficiently consequential, recurring interpretation problem that Hedgr materially improves.

No additional research cycle follows automatically from any disposition.
