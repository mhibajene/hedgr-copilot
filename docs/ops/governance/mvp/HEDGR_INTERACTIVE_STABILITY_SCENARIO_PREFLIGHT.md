## Decision this preparation must enable

Can a participant, after the existing synthetic orientation, explain why a scoped Stability interpretation changes when one or two material circumstances differ, without Founder teaching or assuming real money, comprehensive financial knowledge or actionable advice?

The first experiment will compare orientation plus a small set of deliberately contrasting synthetic cases. It will not introduce user-controlled inputs, personal financial context or a “Use My Numbers” entry. The proposed outcome is clearer causal understanding, not simulator completeness, demand proof or model acceptance.

### Case set and comparison method

All amounts below use **U**, an invented unit with no FX conversion, provider, country, asset price, yield or return. The same fictional owner has one intentional 720 U envelope and 360 U outside it; stocks do not overlap. Observation is Day 0. The envelope cannot be used before Day 20. One stated payment is due Day 10 at an endpoint usable by the outside stock without a fictional fee once that stock is accessible. No alternative source, extension or transfer route is supplied. Other commitments and later needs are not assessed. These stipulated facts are common unless a row says otherwise; they are not authenticated, forecast or applicable to any participant.

| Case | Sole changed circumstance relative to S0 | Supported authored conclusion and reason | Conditions, unknowns and withheld claims |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S0 — reference** | None. Outside 360 U usable Day 0; named payment 280 U due Day 10. | That payment considered alone is covered by the supplied outside stock by its deadline. 360 − 280 = 80 U is arithmetic after that one payment. | The 720 U envelope remains unavailable until Day 20. Other claims are unknown. The 80 U is not free capital, reserve sufficiency or an instruction to pay. |
| **S1 — amount** | The payment is 400 U, still due Day 10. | The supplied timely outside stock is 40 U below this payment: 400 − 360. The unchanged envelope cannot meet the Day 10 timing in the fiction. | Another timely source or extension could change the answer but is not supplied. Do not infer insolvency, overall deterioration or a recommendation to move money. |
| **S2 — access time** | The outside 360 U first becomes usable Day 12; the payment returns to 280 U. | No supplied stock is usable by the Day 10 deadline. The named payment is not covered by the supplied resources at that time, despite unchanged stated ownership. | Day 12 is an authored access assumption, not a guarantee or authenticated record. No bridge is supplied. Do not infer nominal loss or wider resilience. |
| **S3 — missing amount** | The payment exists and is due Day 10, but its amount is withheld; outside access returns to Day 0. | Coverage of that payment cannot be determined. Known stocks, access dates and ownership remain unchanged. | Missing is neither zero nor financial deterioration. Do not calculate a gap, remainder or overall position. The minimum missing fact for this question is the payment amount. |

This is a hub comparison, **S0 → S1, S0 → S2 and S0 → S3**; the contrasts are not cumulative. All three variants keep the same one-obligation question and change exactly one material fact. The set deliberately tests obligation size, timely access and appropriate uncertainty. It does not test currency exposure, irregular inflows or partially protected capital; adding those now would confound the first causal test and require a separate case/semantic decision. The retained PC-01/03/04 cases informed the claim boundary, but these four U/Day values are new fictional stimuli, not new Engine evaluations or independent evidence that the retained model works.

This is a design recommendation only. The later participant-facing ticket must decide and verify the actual display relationship, especially after orientation exposure; no automatic influence, override, shared scoring or executable schema is accepted here.

Claim bundle for every row: the question and one-payment scope; the changed fact; the local conclusion; the supporting amount/timing/access reason; route and fee assumptions; relevant unknowns; withheld broader conclusions; Day 0 observation, Day 10 deadline, resource usable dates and the fact that the interpretation was authored for research. If a compact presentation omits a qualification that changes the claim, omit the stronger claim rather than hiding the qualification in a later screen.

### Observation, coding and disposition

For each case, separate **observed behaviour → first interpretation → post-reveal reflection**. Record voluntary navigation and requests for explanation, first prediction direction (local coverage supported / supplied mismatch / cannot determine / other), named causal fact, scope qualification, synthetic/read-only awareness, personal relevance statement and spontaneous next action. Code correct causal attribution only when the participant names the changed amount, access time or missing amount *and* connects it to the Day 10 question; a vague “Hedgr changed the score” is not success. Preserve verbatim evidence and contrary cases rather than averaging away misunderstanding.

| Element | RETAIN | REVISE / REMOVE | RETEST / ESCALATE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing orientation as common entry | Participants can independently state the simulation and limited-information boundary after exposure. | Revise if orientation repeatedly teaches a broader posture than the cases support; remove only the added experiment's dependence if it prevents the comparison question. | Retest if later prompts or prior exposure make this unclear. Escalate if correcting it requires frozen narrative or trust-boundary change. |
| S0 plus matched variants | Repeated unaided explanations identify the changed fact, local consequence and at least the named-payment scope; participants can describe useful implications without treating the output as advice. | Revise the confusing case/wording if the cause is missed, outputs look arbitrary or precision invites over-trust. Remove a variant if its complexity adds no distinct learning or leads to consistent gaming. | Retest when order, small sample, prior exposure or prompted rescue makes evidence ambiguous. Escalate if a credible conclusion requires executable Engine semantics, broader policy, new input/data or a conflicting posture. |
| Spontaneous next-action question | Retain as a neutral probe only if answers precede capability options and include legitimate non-action. | Revise or remove leading context if participants echo supplied categories rather than volunteering expectations. | Retest demand-related interpretation separately; expectation alone does not prove pull, willingness to use or financial permission. |

These are qualitative decision rules, **not** a claimed sample size, statistical threshold or acceptance of parent `CLASS-A-VAL-002`. The later exposure ticket must lock its sample/cap, unit of analysis, exact stimulus version, coding review method and stop threshold before recruitment. A Founder disposition records each element as RETAIN, REVISE, REMOVE, RETEST or ESCALATE with supporting and contrary observations. Scenario evidence cannot answer whether the current product Engine actually derives a contextual position.
