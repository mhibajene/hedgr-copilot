## 3. Output A — incremental-value hypothesis

### Locked hypothesis

> **This authored scoped interpretation helps participants identify a consequential cross-currency relationship and preserve its uncertainty boundary more accurately than the same facts alone.**

This hypothesis is falsifiable. The study must be capable of showing that the interpretation:

- adds meaningful incremental understanding;
- clarifies something the participant partly understood;
- adds little because the participant already understood the relationship; or
- causes misunderstanding and should be revised or removed.

All four are legitimate outcomes. Positive sentiment, agreement, preferred category language and repetition do not establish incremental utility.

The hypothesis does not establish category uniqueness, competitive superiority, retention, willingness to pay, commercial viability, a complete Stability Position or executable Engine ownership.

## 4. Output B — first-study interpretation contract

Only **Exposure** and **Unknowns** are actively tested. **Purpose** and **Horizon** provide fixed context. **Access** is excluded.

### 4.1 Exposure

| Contract element | Locked content |
| --- | --- |
| Required facts | Sarah's scoped savings are denominated in ZMW. The course obligation is fixed and payable in USD. |
| Permitted inference | A growing kwacha balance alone does not establish how much of the USD obligation it will cover when payment is due, because the relationship between the two currencies matters. |
| Blocked conclusions | Future FX direction; ZMW depreciation; future funding adequacy; guaranteed preservation; need to convert; asset superiority; recommended allocation. |

### 4.2 Unknowns

| Contract element | Locked content |
| --- | --- |
| Required facts | No future exchange rate is supplied. Planned future contributions have not yet occurred. |
| Permitted inference | Future funding adequacy cannot be established from the supplied facts. |
| Blocked conclusions | Guaranteed future contributions; projected final course coverage; guaranteed purchasing power; a complete Stability Position. |

### 4.3 Fixed and excluded dimensions

- **Purpose is fixed context:** the supplied goal is an online postgraduate course. It is not treated as Sarah's complete set of priorities.
- **Horizon is fixed context:** payment is due on 1 October 2027. The study does not compare deadlines or infer that a shorter horizon automatically means more risk.
- **Access is excluded:** no earlier need, holding restriction or accessibility constraint is supplied. Need and capability must not be inferred from one another.

### 4.4 Claim-integrity rule

Every displayed interpretation sentence must be traceable to a supplied fact, a permitted inference and the conclusions that remain blocked. Missing information constrains only dependent conclusions; it does not erase facts that remain known. The study must not turn uncertainty into a forecast, blanket disclaimer or assertion that nothing useful can be concluded.

## 5. Output C — canonical Sarah record

### 5.1 Deterministic factual timeline

**Observation date:** 1 October 2026.

Sarah already has **K6,000** set aside in Zambian kwacha toward an online postgraduate course.

She plans to add **K2,000 on the 15th of each month** from **15 October 2026 through 15 September 2027**. There are **12 planned contributions**, totalling K24,000 if every contribution occurs.

Those contributions:

- have not yet occurred at the observation date;
- are intentions rather than existing capital; and
- are not guaranteed funds.

The provider has fixed the course fee at **USD 1,000**. The fee is:

- payable in US dollars;
- due on **1 October 2027**; and
- due after the final planned contribution date.

The scenario does not supply:

- a future exchange rate;
- conversion costs;
- other financial commitments;
- an earlier need for the scoped money; or
- Sarah's wider financial position.

### 5.2 Arithmetic boundary

K6,000 is the only existing scoped capital on 1 October 2026. K24,000 is a planned total that has not occurred. K30,000 is therefore only a conditional future total if all twelve intended contributions occur; it must never be presented as Sarah's current capital or guaranteed future capital.

### 5.3 Participant-facing narrative rendering

The entire fact set must be understandable as an ordinary money situation before any internal Hedgr ontology appears. A faithful lived-first rendering is:

> **Sarah is saving for a postgraduate course.**
>
> Sarah already has K6,000 set aside. She plans to add K2,000 on the 15th of each month for the next 12 months, starting on 15 October 2026. Those contributions have not happened yet.
>
> The course costs USD 1,000. Payment is due in US dollars on 1 October 2027.

Participant-facing copy must not require familiarity with Exposure, Engine semantics, Stability Position, scoped interpretation or research case ontology.

## 7. Output D — exact authored interpretation

Reveal the following wording exactly after the matched-information baseline:

> **Sarah is saving in kwacha for a course priced in US dollars.**
>
> Her kwacha savings can grow without that alone telling her how much of the USD course fee they will cover when payment is due.
>
> The relationship between the two currencies at that time also matters.
>
> This example cannot predict the future exchange rate, assume Sarah's planned contributions will happen, or establish that the course will be fully funded.

The interpretation follows this order:

1. the person's situation;
2. the financial relationship;
3. why the relationship matters; and
4. what remains unknown.

It is an authored research interpretation. It must not be presented as output computed by the current Stability Engine. Participant-facing output must not lead with Exposure, Stability Position, Engine state, scoped interpretation or an FX-risk score.

## 8. Output E — evidence protocol

### Step 0 — neutral narrative landing

After Sarah's facts are shown, ask exactly:

> **In your own words, what is happening for Sarah?**

No checklist or category prompt follows before the baseline judgement questions. Researchers silently code whether the participant recognises:

- Sarah's intended course goal;
- K6,000 as existing money;
- the K2,000 monthly amounts as future intentions;
- the USD 1,000 obligation; and
- the 1 October 2027 payment date.

This is a stimulus-validity check. Do not evaluate incremental interpretation if the underlying situation is not understandable without moderator rescue. Record the failure source rather than teaching the scenario.

### Step 1 — matched-information baseline

Ask exactly:

> **What, if anything, can you conclude about Sarah's progress toward the course from this information?**

Then ask exactly:

> **What would you still need to know?**

Record the unaided response before revealing the authored interpretation.

### Step 2 — interpretation reveal

Reveal the exact Output D interpretation, then ask exactly:

> **Does this change anything about how you understand Sarah's situation? If so, what?**

The question must permit a legitimate answer of **No**. Do not ask whether Hedgr added value or improved understanding.

### Step 3 — controlled transfer

Change only the course pricing basis:

> **The provider now fixes the course fee at K29,500 payable in Zambian kwacha on 1 October 2027, instead of USD 1,000 payable in US dollars.**

The observation date, K6,000 existing capital, twelve planned K2,000 contributions, contribution dates, payment date and all other supplied facts remain unchanged.

Ask exactly:

> **What changes in your interpretation, and what stays the same?**

Strong transfer evidence includes recognition that:

- the specific future ZMW/USD pricing relationship no longer applies;
- K6,000 remains the only existing scoped capital at observation;
- the twelve K2,000 contributions remain future intentions;
- the payment date is unchanged;
- other unknowns remain unknown; and
- removing the currency mismatch does not establish a complete Stability Position.

A participant may correctly observe that K6,000 plus twelve K2,000 contributions would equal K30,000 and exceed K29,500 by K500 **if every planned contribution occurs**. That conditional reasoning is valid. It must not become “Sarah already has K30,000” or “Sarah will definitely fund the course.”

## 9. Pre-reveal recognition rule

Participants who identify the cross-currency relationship before reveal remain in the study and in the evidence. Code this as **Baseline recognition**.

For these participants, the interpretation may produce:

- no incremental discovery;
- clarification;
- stronger uncertainty discipline; or
- no meaningful additional value.

All are legitimate findings. Existing understanding is evidence about the ceiling of incremental value, not a methodological failure.

## 10. Precommitted incremental-utility rule

Incremental utility requires all of the following where applicable:

1. **Baseline state is preserved.** Record whether the relevant relationship was absent, incomplete or already recognised before reveal.
2. **The post-reveal consequence is correct.** The participant correctly explains why a ZMW balance alone does not establish future coverage of a USD obligation.
3. **The uncertainty boundary remains intact.** The participant does not convert the interpretation into a forecast, guarantee, adequacy claim or instruction.
4. **Transfer is correctly applied.** The participant recognises what changes when the goal becomes fixed in ZMW and what remains unchanged.

Positive sentiment, agreement, preferred vocabulary, task completion and repetition are insufficient.

## 11. Unsupported-inference codes

Precommit the following codes as evidence of misunderstanding even when the participant sounds confident:

- future FX prediction;
- guaranteed preservation;
- assumed depreciation;
- guaranteed funding adequacy;
- future contributions treated as existing capital;
- advice or conversion inference;
- whole-person financial inference;
- unsupported access assumptions; and
- category-language repetition without causal understanding.

Also record whether misunderstanding appears attributable to the financial relationship, scenario facts, research chrome, terminology, visual hierarchy or moderator rescue. This attribution prevents a stimulus-design failure from being misclassified as failure of the interpretation.

## 12. Disposition logic

| Disposition | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **RETAIN** | Participants explain the relevant relationship, preserve its uncertainty boundary and apply it correctly to the transfer case. |
| **REVISE** | The relationship appears useful, but wording, presentation or scenario construction causes avoidable confusion. |
| **REMOVE** | The interpretation adds no meaningful understanding beyond the matched baseline or repeatedly causes false inference. |
| **RETEST** | Prompting, order effects, stimulus defects, participant mismatch or insufficient contrast make the evidence ambiguous. |
| **ESCALATE** | A useful next step requires executable Engine semantics, new data, advice, connectivity, execution or another authority boundary. |

Failure attaches to the tested interpretation or experience. It must not automatically be generalised into failure of Hedgr as a whole.
