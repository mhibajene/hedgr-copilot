# Founder / HedgrOps Position — Institutional Adoption of the Partial-Capital Stability Model

**Status:** Founder / HedgrOps Agreed Strategic Direction — Repo Reconciliation Required  
**Classification:** Founder Only / Internal Product Strategy  
**Scope:** Stability Engine, Wallet MVP, Productisation, GTM interpretation  
**Execution Impact:** None by itself  
**Financial Authority Impact:** None  
**Doctrine Impact:** Clarification of strategic product direction; does not amend canonical doctrine  
**Implementation Authority:** Must continue to derive from `HEDGR_STATUS.md`, `AGENTS.md`, accepted ADRs and active repo governance

---

## 1. Institutional Position

Hedgr adopts the **partial-capital Stability Engine model** as the strategic functional frame for MVP productisation.

The institution does not assume that a user's full income, available capital or wider financial life must reside within Hedgr for the product to create value.

Instead, Hedgr should be capable of helping a user understand:

- their relevant financial context;
- what must remain available for ordinary financial life;
- what portion of capital they intentionally place under Hedgr's stability reasoning;
- how that capital should be understood across liquidity, stability and subordinate yield;
- what has materially changed;
- and what options may be available without unnecessarily weakening their financial resilience.

The core principle is:

> **Hedgr should create meaningful stability intelligence without requiring users to centralise their entire financial life within Hedgr.**

---

## 2. Working MVP Audience

The institution retains the working MVP audience hypothesis of:

> **Financially active earners — initially freelancers, independent professionals and salaried professionals — who receive recurring income, possess meaningful financial agency, and make recurring decisions about liquidity, preservation, spending and allocation.**

Employment structure is not itself the product boundary.

The more important common characteristic is:

> **The user has money available and faces a consequential decision about what that money should do.**

This audience hypothesis remains reversible and subject to later behavioural validation through real MVP use.

It is not a claim of demonstrated product-market fit.

---

## 3. Functional Product Thesis

The Stability Engine exists to help answer:

> **What is my financial position, how resilient is it, what changed, and what can I responsibly consider doing next?**

For MVP productisation, the institution should therefore distinguish between:

### Wider financial context

The user's income, commitments, operating requirements, timing and other relevant financial conditions.

Hedgr does not need to hold all of this capital.

### Hedgr allocation envelope

The portion of capital the user intentionally places under Hedgr's stability reasoning.

This is the capital Hedgr is being asked to help interpret and manage from a stability perspective.

### Stability composition

Within that envelope, Hedgr should reason about the roles of:

- **Liquidity** — capital that must remain accessible;
- **Stability** — capital whose primary role is preservation and resilience;
- **Yield** — subordinate capital for which conservative return-seeking may eventually be appropriate under authorised policy.

The constitutional ordering remains:

> **Preservation and liquidity before yield.**

---

## 4. Institutional Product Model

The target functional model is:

> **Financial event → Operating requirement → Hedgr allocation envelope → Liquidity requirement → Stability reserve → Yield capacity → Exposure / change interpretation → Safe options**

This should be treated as a product architecture model rather than a commitment that every component must immediately appear as a feature or UI control.

The institution should avoid prematurely translating the model into separate product modules.

---

## 5. Stability Engine Functional Primitives

Productisation should progressively converge around the following functional primitives.

### Financial context
What relevant financial circumstances surround the Hedgr position?

### Allocation envelope
What portion of capital is intentionally being considered through Hedgr?

### Commitments
What capital is effectively already spoken for?

### Liquidity
What must remain readily accessible?

### Stability
What capital's primary purpose is resilience and preservation?

### Exposure
What material financial risks affect the position?

### Time
What matters now, soon or before the next expected financial event?

### Capacity
What remains available for consideration without unnecessarily undermining resilience?

### Change
What has materially changed in the user's position?

### Options and consequences
What might the user consider doing and what would that imply?

### Reason and uncertainty
Why is the Engine presenting this interpretation, and what relevant information does it not know?

These are architectural primitives.

They are not an instruction to create eleven product features.

---

## 6. MVP Productisation Principle

Each authorised MVP increment should increasingly answer:

> **Which part of the Stability Engine's functional model are we making real, understandable and testable next?**

Productisation should not be driven primarily by feature completeness.

Priority should go to capabilities that improve the user's understanding of:

- financial position;
- relevant commitments;
- liquidity and resilience;
- material change;
- stability capacity;
- safe available options.

Features that add breadth without strengthening this reasoning loop should generally remain subordinate.

---

## 7. Partial Capital as the Default Adoption Assumption

The MVP should be useful even when the user places only part of their available capital within Hedgr.

For example, a salaried professional may retain substantial operating money in an existing bank or mobile-money account while allocating another portion toward Hedgr.

A freelancer may similarly:

- withdraw part of incoming income for near-term spending;
- retain part as accessible liquidity;
- place part under longer-term stability management.

No fixed percentage is implied.

Any examples such as 60/40 are illustrative only.

Hedgr should not optimise for deposit capture or encourage users to unnecessarily centralise funds.

---

## 8. Two Forms of Liquidity

The institution recognises an important conceptual distinction:

### External operating liquidity

Capital deliberately maintained outside Hedgr because existing financial infrastructure remains the user's practical spending environment.

### Hedgr liquidity

Capital within the Hedgr allocation envelope that nevertheless needs to remain highly accessible or withdrawal-ready.

For MVP, external operating liquidity may remain substantial.

Future payment or card capabilities may allow more operating liquidity to remain inside Hedgr, but this should extend the functional model rather than require its redesign.

---

## 9. Implication for the Wallet

The Wallet should be understood as:

> **The experience and eventual action surface for the portion of the user's financial position entrusted to Hedgr's stability system.**

The Wallet does not define Hedgr's product logic.

The Stability Engine remains the interpretive centre.

The Wallet should consume and express Engine reasoning rather than independently create financial logic.

---

## 10. Implication for Future Capability

The partial-capital model creates a progressive path.

### MVP

Hedgr may operate alongside the user's existing financial infrastructure.

### Later phases

Subject to separate governance and evidence, Hedgr may progressively support:

- direct income receipt;
- richer liquidity management;
- cards or payment capabilities;
- broader account connectivity;
- approved conversion;
- approved stability allocation;
- conservative yield execution.

Those capabilities could allow more of the user's financial operating life to occur inside Hedgr.

They are **not prerequisites for the Stability Engine thesis**.

The institution should therefore earn progressively greater financial responsibility rather than require financial centralisation at adoption.

---

## 11. Product Experience Implication

The canonical journey remains:

> **Entry → Orientation → Stability → Insight → Safe action**

The functional interpretation becomes:

### Entry
What financial event or decision brought the user to Hedgr?

### Orientation
What part of the user's position is Hedgr evaluating?

### Stability
How resilient is the relevant position?

### Insight
What changed or materially matters?

### Safe action
What might the user responsibly consider doing next?

This deepens the existing journey rather than replacing it.

---

## 12. Behavioural Validation Posture

The institution will not gate current MVP productisation on proving the behavioural market in advance.

The working audience and partial-capital model should instead become testable through a sufficiently coherent MVP.

Once users can experience Hedgr, the institution should increasingly observe:

- what triggers use;
- what users understand;
- what users misunderstand;
- whether stability interpretation provides incremental value;
- whether insights affect real decisions;
- whether users return around subsequent financial events;
- whether freelancers and salaried professionals exhibit the same underlying decision grammar.

Formal behavioural-market validation is deferred, not abandoned.

Observed behaviour should eventually determine whether the audience hypothesis is retained, narrowed, broadened or split.

---

## 13. Explicit Non-Adoptions

Institutional adoption of this direction does **not** mean that Hedgr is adopting:

- a budgeting product;
- full financial aggregation;
- transaction categorisation;
- autonomous financial planning;
- credit scoring;
- predictive income modelling;
- cards as an MVP dependency;
- automatic reallocation;
- investment management;
- live portfolio optimisation;
- separate freelancer and professional products;
- three literal user-controlled pots corresponding to every allocation band;
- a mandatory percentage of income that should enter Hedgr.

Nor does it authorise:

- custody;
- customer-money operations;
- live conversion;
- financial settlement;
- yield execution;
- card issuance;
- provider commitments;
- new rails;
- market launch;
- widening of current Class A / B / C authority.

---

## 14. Guardrails for Productisation

The institution should resist product expansion where:

- implementation breadth begins outrunning Stability Engine clarity;
- users must provide their entire financial life for Hedgr to be useful;
- budgeting mechanics begin replacing stability reasoning;
- yield becomes more prominent than liquidity or preservation;
- precision exceeds what the underlying data can support;
- informational outputs begin appearing as accounting truth;
- recommendations become directive rather than advisory;
- wallet functionality begins defining the Engine rather than consuming it.

If any of these conditions emerge, the affected work should be revised or stopped.

---

## 15. Adoption Test for Future Product Work

A proposed MVP capability should be evaluated against the following question:

> **Does this materially improve Hedgr's ability to understand or communicate financial context, the user's stability allocation, liquidity and resilience, material change, or safe capacity?**

If yes, it is plausibly strengthening the core product.

If it primarily increases transactional breadth, financial complexity or feature completeness without improving that reasoning loop, it should normally remain outside the immediate productisation priority.

## 17. Institutional Adoption Disposition

### ADOPT

The **partial-capital Stability Engine model** as Hedgr's functional productisation direction.

### RETAIN

The Stability Engine as product and interpretive centre.

The Wallet as initial experience / eventual action surface.

The financially active earner as the working MVP audience hypothesis.

The constitutional priority of preservation, liquidity and visible risk.

### CLARIFY

Hedgr need not own or hold the user's entire financial position to provide meaningful stability intelligence.

### DEFER

Formal behavioural-market proof until meaningful MVP exposure provides stronger evidence.

### DO NOT INFER

Any new implementation, financial execution, market, card, provider or integration authority.

## 19. Closing Institutional Position

> **Hedgr is a financial stability system for people who already have financial agency but need better intelligence about what their available money should do.**

> **The product should be useful before Hedgr becomes the centre of the user's financial life.**

> **The Stability Engine should understand relevant context, reason over the capital deliberately placed under its stability model, preserve liquidity and resilience first, and help the user understand what can responsibly happen next.**

> **As trust, evidence and authorised capability grow, Hedgr may assume greater financial responsibility. The architecture should support that progression without requiring it for MVP value.**

This is the strategic direction the institution should now productise against.
