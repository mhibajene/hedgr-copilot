## 1. Purpose

Create one internal Research R1 orientation document that helps a sophisticated evaluator understand Hedgr’s problem, category, system centre, institutional posture, current implementation boundary, bounded evidence, and long-term vision.

This is orientation, not persuasion. It is not an investor deck, pitch deck, fundraising deck, white paper, investment memorandum, marketing campaign, offer, or solicitation.

## 2. Editorial outcome

The document should feel calm, premium, institutional, modern, understated, and human. It should use Japanese editorial restraint, Swiss hierarchy, generous negative space, and a disciplined financial-infrastructure tone.

The reader should finish with:

> I understand Hedgr.

The artifact must not require or reward crypto expertise.

## 3. Source and truth rules

- Preserve the accepted source meaning and sentence rhythm.
- Do not add positioning, product capability, claims, traction, or evidence.
- Distinguish current implementation, bounded evidence, institutional intent, and future vision.
- Keep the current boundary visible: synthetic and informational Class A work; no live financial service or customer-money movement.
- Where the source is silent, the design must remain silent.
- Diagrams may clarify relationships already expressed in the source; they may not create technical architecture or execution semantics.

## 4. Format and page architecture

Use A4 portrait to retain document character and continuity with the Customer One-Pager. Maximum eight pages total:

1. **Cover** — title, Research R1 designation, official Hedgr logo, restrained institutional framing.
2. **The Financial Stability Problem** — financial-system limitation, volatility exposure, and the stability gap.
3. **A New Category** — Stability Wallet and the four ordered priorities.
4. **What Hedgr Is** — Financial Stability Operating System, Stability Wallet, Stability Engine, and governance.
5. **Institutional Philosophy** — trust posture and `Preservation → Liquidity → Understanding → Yield`.
6. **What Exists Today** — institutional, governance, and product foundations; present boundary; intentionally unbuilt capability.
7. **Evidence** — bounded evidence categories and what remains unproven.
8. **Long-Term Vision + Evaluation** — Wallet, Pro, and API surfaces downstream of one governed core; closing evaluation frame.

Long-Term Vision and Evaluation share page 8. The artifact must not become nine pages.

## 6. Grid and typography

Use a six-column A4 portrait grid with:

- 18–22 mm outer margins;
- 4–5 mm gutters;
- a 4 mm baseline rhythm;
- one primary text measure spanning three to four columns;
- one narrow annotation rail where useful;
- no dense slide-style card wall.

Suggested hierarchy:

- Cover title: 34–42 pt, Plus Jakarta Sans, medium or semibold.
- Section statement: 28–34 pt, Plus Jakarta Sans, medium.
- Section label: 8–10 pt, uppercase or small caps treatment, tracked.
- Body: 10–11.5 pt, Inter or Plus Jakarta Sans, regular.
- Caption / source note: 7.5–9 pt.
- Pull statement: 15–20 pt with generous leading.

## 7. Visual system

Anchor the layout in the logo’s bold geometric forms and strong diagonals. Use cropped triangular fields, angled transitions, and negative-space cut-outs sparingly. The document should remain recognisably Hedgr even when the logo is temporarily removed.

Use diagrams for:

- the gap between efficient financial infrastructure and resilient outcomes;
- the ordered priorities;
- Wallet as delivery surface, Engine as system centre, governance as capability control;
- current foundations versus deliberately unauthorised capability;
- one governed core flowing to future Wallet, Pro, and API surfaces.

Avoid charts, KPI dashboards, growth curves, market maps, or technical architecture.
