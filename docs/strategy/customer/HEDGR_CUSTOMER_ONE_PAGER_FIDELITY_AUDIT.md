## Cross-Section Findings

### 1. The opening already leads with human context

The title and problem sections operate primarily at Fidelity 1. They do not force the reader to understand crypto, DeFi, governance, or system architecture first. The prior review's positive readability finding remains valid evidence.

### 2. The main fidelity interruption occurs inside `What Hedgr is`

That section moves from Hedgr's human role to institutional machinery and then back to the first product representation. The issue is structural sequencing, not simply technical vocabulary. A first-time reader is asked to hold `Financial Stability Operating System` and `Stability Wallet` before the role has fully settled.

### 3. Necessary boundaries carry real cognitive cost

`What is real today` is dense because it protects material truth. That detail should not be removed merely to make the brief feel simpler. Any future revision should improve the path into the boundary and its disclosure order while preserving every exclusion.

### 4. Two phrases deserve claim-risk review

- `what action is safely available next`
- `Consider a safe next action`

Both can be read as Hedgr certifying an action as safe. The intended meaning is clarity before an available next step, not financial advice or a guarantee. This audit does not supply replacement copy.

### 5. Product machinery is optional for first understanding

The system identity and product-surface name may be useful, but they are not necessary to answer the first customer questions: why this matters, what Hedgr helps with, and what is real today. Their placement should be justified by comprehension evidence, not institutional importance alone.
